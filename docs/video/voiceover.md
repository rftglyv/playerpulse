## Paste into ElevenLabs (v3), one take

```
[curious, warm] After every patch, players flood Steam, Discord and in-game chat, in Azerbaijani, Russian and English.
[short pause]
[confident] PlayerPulse reads every message, checks it against your gameplay telemetry, and gives each complaint a verdict.
[short pause]
[proud, measured] Test pack: five of five planted bugs, zero false alarms. Keyword filters? Fifty-nine percent.
[short pause]
[friendly] No guesswork, no spreadsheets. Let's open the live product.
[short pause]
[calm] Every studio signs in to its own workspace. Your players' data stays yours.
[short pause]
[intrigued] Here's the whole patch at a glance. Look at level four: completion fell from eighty percent to forty-three, while deaths stayed flat.
[short pause]
[serious] Flat deaths with a collapsing finish rate is not a difficulty problem. That's a blocker bug.
[short pause]
[decisive] Ranked by players lost, not by noise.
[short pause]
[focused, clear] Number one: Twin Bridges, two thousand nine hundred forty-six players lost. The cause is quoted from the patch notes, and every claim links back to the player's own words, translated.
[short pause]
[reassuring, slight smile] The 'level three is impossible' crowd? Completion eighty-five to eighty-four. Dismissed, with proof.
[short pause]
[upbeat] Real issues become tasks, tagged P-zero to P-two, ready for your board.
[short pause]
[warm] Every player still heard, grouped by what they're talking about. About twenty cents per hundred messages.
[short pause]
[confident] Thirty seconds a run.
[short pause]
[excited, punchy] Player complaints in. Real bugs out. This is PlayerPulse.
[short pause]
```

Or generate one clip per cue and drop each at its start time in CapCut; the timings above are the cue starts.
